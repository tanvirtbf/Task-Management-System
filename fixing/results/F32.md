## 2. The sweep's 9 red files, decoded

The 30-module serial sweep showed 9 files red. Applying the F23 protocol — **a red row in a serial
chain is not evidence until re-run solo** — split them cleanly:

**Seven were pool flake** (green in isolation, the shared-box draining-pool signature that has
recurred every phase): `customfields/list` 13/13 · `deptreview/head-assignment`+`reports-actions`
24/24 · `lists/create` 60/60 · `membership/assignees.remove` 19/19 · `rbac/can` 96/96 ·

**Two were genuinely stale — and instructively so.** They were not fixing-phase regressions; they
were collateral from a **parallel overdue-alerts build** that landed `upgrades/014` mid-sweep,
re-adding the `overdue` notification type (D6 had removed it as producerless; the new overdue-alert
job is a real producer, so this is a legitimate re-add). Early sweep modules saw 7 notification
types, late ones saw 8 — the exact flake signature of a schema change racing a long test run:

- `notifications/preferences` — a `422 for a dropped type (overdue)` case that is no longer a
  dropped type. Case removed with a note.
- `dept-review/notifications-ux` — a hardcoded 7-type pinned list. Updated to 8.

Both green after the update (17/17, 3/3). The lesson worth keeping: when two builds share a test
DB, a mid-run migration reads exactly like a flake — check the schema clock, not just the pool.
